# Structure — write the paper the venue actually prints

Structure is not taste. It is a measurable property of the venue, and the
measurement takes an afternoon.

## Read eight exemplars and count

For the target venue, take recent papers of the kind yours will be and count:

- **theorems** — one venue's empirical-and-architecture papers had a median of
  **zero**; its theory papers had a median of eighteen;
- **tables** — the same split: one against eight;
- **references** — 56 for theory, 88 for empirical, 159 for surveys;
- **where the contribution is stated** — first paragraph, end of introduction,
  or nowhere explicit.

Those three paradigms are different papers. Writing an architecture paper with
theory-paper proportions produces something that reads as thin theory rather
than as a strong empirical result.

## The page-count trap

Exemplars collected from preprint servers are **author-formatted and routinely
over the venue's official limit** — in one sample, five of eight were, several at
twice it. They are not the published versions.

**Learn structure from exemplars. Learn length from the call for papers.** This
error is easy to make because the exemplars look typeset.

## Contribution type decides more than topic

Measured on one venue: the largest contribution category was "training and
optimisation strategy" at 19%, far larger than "new architecture"; and the
subfield a particular programme was strongest in occupied 2%. The *same work*
framed the second way lands in a tenfold larger slice. See
[`../10-direction/venue-fit.md`](../10-direction/venue-fit.md).

Decide which category yours is **before** drafting, and write the introduction
for that category. This is the single highest-leverage structural decision and
it is usually made by default.

## The introduction's job

Four sentences, in this order: what is true today, what it fails at, what you do,
what the number is. Anything before the first sentence is throat-clearing, and a
reviewer's attention budget is spent by the end of the first page.

## What goes in the appendix

Everything a reader would only consult to check you: full hyperparameters,
derivations, the ablations that confirm rather than discriminate. Not the
evidence for a main claim — a main claim whose evidence lives in the appendix
reads as a claim whose evidence did not survive the page limit.

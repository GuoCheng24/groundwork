# Venue fit — capacity is a separate gate from novelty

A direction can be genuinely unoccupied **and** have almost no room to be
published, and the two are correlated: some areas are empty *because* the venues
that would take them are small. Unoccupied is one gate. Capacity is another.

## Measure the venue instead of guessing it

The standard move, once, per target venue:

1. Collect 200–300 recent titles, and abstracts where you can get them.
2. Tabulate **topic** × **contribution type**.
3. Decide which cell your work lands in.
4. If it only lands in a cell under ~3%, either accept that it must be the
   benchmark paper of that subfield, or reframe the contribution.

## The finding that makes this worth doing

On one major venue, measured across 300 titles and 191 abstracts:

- the three areas a particular programme had a real advantage in occupied
  **2.0%, 1.3% and 1.0%** of the issue — single digits of papers a year;
- but the largest **contribution type** was "training and optimisation
  strategy" at **19%**, far larger than "new architecture";
- and inside the 2% subfield itself, the most common contribution type was
  *still* training/optimisation strategy.

**The same work framed as "uncertainty and calibration" lands in a 2% slice;
framed as "a training strategy with guarantees" it lands in a 19% one.** That is
a tenfold difference in available room, from a decision about framing rather
than about content. Contribution type predicts acceptance better than topic
does, and almost nobody measures it.

## Two counting conventions that must not be mixed

**Dominant topic** (each paper counted once) and **involvement rate** (papers
may count in several) give very different numbers for the same corpus: 2.0%
against 13.1% for the same area. Both are meaningful and they answer different
questions —

- lead-topic share answers *can this be the paper's headline?*
- involvement share answers *can this be a component of somebody else's paper?*

A subject that is 2% as a lead and 13% as a component is telling you exactly
which of the two routes exists.

## The page-count trap

Exemplar papers collected from preprint servers are usually **author-formatted
and over the venue's official limit** — several at twice it. Learn structure
from exemplars; never learn length from them. Length comes from the official
call.

## What to record

The venue profile, in a form the next project can reuse: topic distribution,
contribution-type distribution, the cross-tabulation, and the three paradigms
the venue actually prints (theory papers, empirical papers, surveys) with their
typical table counts, theorem counts and reference counts. This is research
infrastructure, not a one-off.

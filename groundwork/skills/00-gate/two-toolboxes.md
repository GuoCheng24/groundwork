# Two toolboxes — where originality actually comes from

A strategic gate, upstream of the numerical one. It decides what is worth
proposing at all, and it is uncomfortable.

## The claim

Original work comes from **bridging two domains you are deep in**. Not one deep
domain applied harder, and not two shallow ones combined.

If you are deep in exactly one domain — which is the normal case — then
everything you can build is *deep × shallow*: your real expertise applied to a
field you understand at reading level. That work is real and publishable, and it
has a specific vulnerability: **the shallow side can be replaced by somebody who
is deep in it.** An insider in that second field, given your idea, does it
better and faster. Reviewers from that field can tell, and they are the ones
assigned.

## The question the gate asks

> **Which two deep domains is this a bridge between?**

An answer naming one domain plus an application area is a deep × shallow
project. That is not a refusal — most work is this — but it changes what you
should expect and how you should defend it:

- the contribution must be the *bridge*, stated explicitly, not the application;
- the shallow side needs a collaborator who is deep in it, early, not at review;
- do not compete on the shallow side's own terms.

## The uncomfortable implication

When you only have one toolbox, **investing in a second one beats investing in
another project**. A year spent becoming genuinely deep in a second domain
changes what you can propose for the following decade; a year spent on another
deep × shallow project does not.

Most research programmes cannot act on this, which is exactly why it is worth
knowing. A pipeline that only optimises the next paper will never surface it —
so it is written here, at the gate, where the question is asked before the work.

## How this interacts with the numerical gate

They are independent and both are necessary:

- `00-gate/SKILL.md` asks *can this be measured to matter?*
- this asks *is this a thing only we can do?*

A direction can pass the numbers and fail here — a clean measurable question in
a field where an insider will answer it first. And it can pass here and fail the
numbers, which is the cheaper failure and the reason the numbers come first in
wall-clock order.

## What to record

The answer to the bridge question, in one sentence, in the project's first
commit. It is the sentence you will defend in every review, and writing it early
makes the drift visible when the project quietly becomes something else.

# Rebuttal — answer the review that was written

## The shape of one response

For every point, in this order:

1. **the reviewer's sentence, quoted.** Not paraphrased. A paraphrase lets you
   answer a weaker version, and the reviewer will notice;
2. **whether they are right.** Say so plainly when they are. A rebuttal that
   concedes nothing reads as a rebuttal that read nothing, and the conceded
   points are what buy credibility for the contested ones;
3. **what changed, with the number and the location.** "Added Table 4; the gap
   is 2.1 points, Section 5.2";
4. **what did not change, and why.** In one sentence.

## Reframing beats re-running, more often than feels honest

When a weakness can be addressed by stating the claim correctly, that is usually
the *right* fix rather than the cheap one: the original sentence was
overreaching, and the reviewer noticed. A new experiment that defends an
overreaching sentence is a new experiment defending a sentence you should not
make.

Reserve new runs for points where the reviewer is asking a genuine empirical
question you cannot answer from what you have.

**And never promise an experiment you have not run.** A promised experiment
becomes a condition of acceptance and then an obligation at a worse moment.

## Correcting a reviewer who is wrong

One sentence, the fact, the location. Not three paragraphs. Length reads as
defensiveness and the area chair is skimming.

If the reviewer is wrong *because the paper was unclear*, that is a paper
problem. Say "we see how the original wording suggested X" and fix the wording —
this is the most common case and the one authors most often mishandle.

## The review is also data about the writing

Collect, across all reviewers, every point where somebody stumbled. Those
locations are defects regardless of whether the objection was correct: two
reviewers misreading the same sentence is a fact about the sentence.

## Before sending

- every reviewer point has a numbered response, including the ones you disagree
  with;
- every claim of a change names the section and the number;
- the limitations that came out of the loop are **in the manuscript**, not only
  in the response letter. A limitation that lives only in a rebuttal disappears
  on acceptance;
- read the whole thing once as the reviewer. The tone that feels firm while
  writing usually reads as brittle.

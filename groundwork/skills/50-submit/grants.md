# Proposals — reviewers fund feasibility, not ideas

A funding proposal and a paper are judged on different things, and the mistake
that costs most is bringing a paper's habits to a proposal.

A paper argues **that a result is true**. A proposal argues **that you will
produce a result** — so the evidence a proposal needs is evidence about *you*
and *this plan*, not about the idea.

## The gate applies here first, and harder

Everything in [`../00-gate/SKILL.md`](../00-gate/SKILL.md) applies before a
proposal is written, not after:

- **the ceiling.** A proposal whose oracle ceiling is closed is a proposal to
  spend somebody's money finding that out. Measure it and put the measurement in
  as preliminary data — a reviewer who sees that you checked is being told
  something about you as much as about the project.
- **occupancy.** A proposal for something already done is the single most common
  desk rejection, and a reviewer in the field will know within a page.
- **capacity and fit.** Read what the scheme actually funds — not its stated
  scope, but its recently funded list, the same way you read a venue's recent
  papers. The distribution is the scope.

## "Why you" is a real question with a real answer

The reviewer's question is not "is this interesting" but "why is this team the
one to do it". The honest answers are narrow:

- **an asset nobody else has** — a cohort, an instrument, a dataset, an access
  agreement;
- **a capability demonstrated by output**, with the output cited;
- **a bridge between two areas you are genuinely deep in.** See
  [`../00-gate/two-toolboxes.md`](../00-gate/two-toolboxes.md), which is the
  same question in its most literal form: if the answer is one deep area plus an
  application field, name the collaborator who is deep in the second one, in the
  proposal, by name.

An answer that amounts to "we are competent and interested" is the answer of
every unsuccessful proposal.

## Preliminary data is the proposal

The section that decides the outcome is the one showing the method already works
in miniature. It is not a summary of past work; it is the smallest version of the
proposed thing, with a number.

Which means the preliminary experiment should be designed the way any experiment
is — pre-registered, with a stopping rule and the sentence that gets written if
it comes out the other way. A preliminary result you would have reported either
way is worth far more than one you selected.

## A plan a reviewer can believe

- **Milestones with decision points**, not a list of activities. Each milestone
  says what would make you change course, which is the part that reads as
  competence rather than optimism.
- **A risk section that names a real risk** and the fallback. Every proposal has
  one; the ones that name it are the ones that get believed. "The main risk is
  that the effect is smaller than our preliminary estimate; the fallback is the
  characterisation study, which is publishable regardless" is a sentence that
  wins arguments in a panel.
- **A budget that matches the plan.** Compute and storage estimated from a
  measured run rather than guessed; staff time that adds up to the milestones.
- **Data management, ethics and sharing** stated concretely. These are scored,
  and vague answers are marked as vague.

## The defence, and auditing your own proposal

Where the scheme includes a proposal defence or an internal review, treat the
document exactly like a manuscript going through
[`../30-claim/SKILL.md`](../30-claim/SKILL.md):

1. **every number traceable** to a committed file — preliminary results,
   cohort sizes, timings, budget lines;
2. **a reader with no context**, from a different model, asked *what the
   evidence supports* rather than asked to confirm the plan;
3. **the figures audited at the size they will be seen** — a panel reads a
   printed copy, sometimes projected.

The defects are the same defects: a correct number in a sentence that does not
follow, a figure whose visual conclusion is backwards, a capability claimed in
the narrative that the preliminary data does not demonstrate.

## What to reuse

A proposal, a paper and a patent disclosure share a claim list and disagree
about what counts as evidence. Keep the claim list once — see
[`../40-write/claims.md`](../40-write/claims.md) — and write the evidence column
three times.

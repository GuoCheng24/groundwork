# Resubmission — a rejected paper is not a template change

The failure is to port the same claim to a new venue with a new style file. The
claim that failed once will fail again, usually for the same reason, and now
with a year gone.

## Start from the claim, not the manuscript

1. Collect every review. Group the objections by **what they are about**, not by
   reviewer: the claim, the evidence, the framing, the writing.
2. Objections about the **claim** are the ones that matter. If two reviewers at
   different venues both said the contribution is incremental, the contribution
   is incremental *as stated*, and no amount of rewriting the introduction fixes
   a claim problem.
3. Decide what this version claims, and write that sentence down before editing
   anything.

## Then re-run the venue gate on the new claim

[`../10-direction/venue-fit.md`](../10-direction/venue-fit.md), against the new
target: which contribution category does this claim fall into, how much room
does that category have, and is this a lead-topic paper or a component paper
there?

The same work framed two ways has landed in a 2% slice and a 19% one at the same
venue. A rejection is the cheapest possible moment to make that decision
deliberately, because it is the one moment the sunk cost is already sunk.

## What actually gets rewritten

- **the introduction**, entirely, around the new claim;
- **the contribution list**, to match the category;
- **the related work**, to the new venue's community — the same field has
  different canonical references in different rooms;
- **the limitations**, to include what the previous reviewers found. A
  limitation raised by a rejecting reviewer and absent from the resubmission is
  the fastest possible rejection at the next venue, because reviewers overlap.

## What does not get rewritten

The results. If the resubmission needs different numbers, this is not a
resubmission; it is a new paper and the gate applies again.

## Carry the previous reviews as a checklist

Every point that two reviewers raised independently is a defect in the work or
in the writing. Before submitting, walk the list and mark each one addressed,
consciously declined, or still open. The still-open ones go into the limitations
section in your own words.

## Timing and the archive

If a preprint is public, a claim change is visible. Decide whether the preprint
is updated at resubmission or at acceptance, and be consistent about it — and
remember that for anything with a patent track, publication is prior art. See
[`patent.md`](patent.md).

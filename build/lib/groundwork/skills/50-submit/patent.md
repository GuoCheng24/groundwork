# Patents — the ordering constraints that cannot be undone

A patent track shares almost nothing with a paper track except the text. What it
adds is a set of ordering constraints, and every one of them is irreversible.

## Publication is prior art against your own application

A paper, a preprint, a talk or a public repository describing the invention
becomes prior art the moment it is public — in most jurisdictions against your
own later filing. **File first, or accept that you have chosen not to.** That
decision is made once, early, deliberately, and written down, because by the
time anyone notices it is a decision it has already been made by default.

## Claims can only be broadened before filing

Dependent and weighted claims must be supported by the specification, and
support cannot be added afterwards. So:

- read the specification against every claim **before** filing, not after;
- anything you might want to claim later must be described now;
- a claim with no basis in the specification is not a narrow claim, it is an
  invalid one.

## An error in the disclosure propagates

An agent drafting from your invention-disclosure document inherits its mistakes,
and a technical error there — a direction of an inequality, a sign, what a mask
excludes — survives into the claims where it is expensive. One such error
originated in the disclosure and reached a filed draft.

**Audit the disclosure as an artifact in its own right** before anything is
generated from it, with the same three layers `30-claim` uses on a paper.

## Novelty search is not the same search

Patent novelty is assessed against a different corpus and a different standard
from academic novelty. A mechanism can be unpublished in the literature and
squarely inside an existing patent family. Search the patent corpus separately,
and do not let an academic occupancy result stand in for it.

## What an agent should and should not do here

Drafting, restructuring, prior-art search, consistency checking between claims
and specification: yes, and it is good at them.

Deciding what to claim, when to file, and in which jurisdictions: no. Those are
legal decisions with deadlines that cannot be re-run, and the correct behaviour
is to surface them to a person with the constraint stated plainly.
